 SWORN  a hook can quote one price and charge another
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

  WHAT THIS IS
  Uniswap v4 lets anyone attach code to a pool. That code runs inside the
  swap, and it can tell a price quote apart from a real trade, because
  tx.gasprice is 0 under eth_call and non-zero in a transaction. So a hook
  can promise one number to every quoting engine on the market and pay out
  a smaller one to the person actually trading.

  Sworn is a router that closes that. It re-quotes every candidate route
  inside the transaction that settles, takes the best, and reverts if what
  executed differs from what it probed.

  WHAT THIS DEMO PROVES, IN THREE PARTS

  PART 1  Can a hook do this at all?
          A fixture hook, deployed locally, that quotes honestly to a
          simulator and then charges a real transaction.
          you will see:  the quote, the payout, and the gap between them
          runs on:       a bare local EVM, no network, ~20 seconds

  PART 2  Does it happen for real, and can it be stopped?
          A hook that is live on Base right now, at a pinned block, sent
          the identical swap from two different callers.
          you will see:  two different payouts for the same trade, and
                         Sworn settling elsewhere for more
          runs on:       Base, forked locally, ~60 seconds

  PART 3  How often, and who is exposed?
          The measurement the first two parts rest on.
          you will see:  how many hooks charge more than they quote, out
                         of those with enough fills to judge, and how many
                         swaps went into them
          runs on:       committed result files, rendered statically

  Nothing below is typed in. Every figure is produced during this run, and
  the gate greps the source to prove no console.log string contains one.
